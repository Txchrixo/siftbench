Campaign methodology

How the inputs were fixed, how findings were graded and what the scores can support.

Inputs and isolation

Each tool received the same 50 code-only changes at pinned base and head revisions. The reference rubric stayed in the evaluator workspace. The campaign used 120 repositories, with 40 assigned exclusively to each reviewer. Some repositories held multiple PRs for the same tool, so prior reviews or public upstream history may have influenced later results.

The audit verified all 150 captured reviews against GitHub. Four interrupted captures were recovered from their original PRs without triggering another review.

Grading and agreement

Initial verdicts were AI-assisted against the frozen rubric. Three Claude models assessed the same claims separately with reviewer labels removed, although wording could still reveal the source. They agreed unanimously on $unanimous of $annotated ($agreement_percent%), with Fleiss’ κ of $kappa$info_agreement. The models share a family and can share systematic errors.

Disagreements were adjudicated against the pinned sources. Two PRs with unresolved findings remain in coverage. Scores use the $scored_prs fully graded cases shared by all three reviewers.

Limits and reproducibility

There was one run per reviewer and change. Review cost was not measured, and the scored cases had no graded high- or critical-severity references. The results describe this campaign and its reviewer settings.
